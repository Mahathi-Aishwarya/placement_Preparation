# English Lesson 1: Self-introduction, fillers, and the most repeated HR questions

## 1. Why this matters

"Tell me about yourself" opens almost every interview and every HR round. The first 60 to 90 seconds set the tone. Interviewers are not testing your accent. They are checking three things: **clarity**, **structure** and **confidence**.

## 2. The structure: Present -> Past -> Future (+ one proof point)

| Part | Purpose | What to include |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | Who you are now | Name, year, college, branch, CGPA |
| **Past / proof** | Evidence you can do things | 1 to 2 achievements: a project, an award, an internship |
| **Skills now** | What you are building | The tools or skills linked to the role you want |
| **Future** | What you want | The role, and why it suits you |

Length: **60 to 90 seconds**, which is about 150 to 200 words at a calm pace.

## 3. A fill-in template

> Good morning. My name is **[name]**. I'm a **[year]** **[degree and branch]** student at **[college]**, with a CGPA of **[x]**.
>
> I'm interested in **[field]**, and I'm aiming for **[role]** roles. My project **[project name]** **[what it does and what it achieved]**. I was also part of **[team or activity]**, where I **[your contribution]**.
>
> These experiences taught me to **[skill 1]** and **[skill 2]**. Right now I'm building hands-on skills in **[tools]** by **[how you practise]**. I'm looking for a role where I can keep learning and contribute from day one. Thank you.

**A worked example (fictional):**

> Good morning. My name is Ravi Kumar. I'm a final-year B.Tech IT student at ABC Institute of Technology, with a CGPA of 8.2.
>
> I'm interested in networking and system administration, and I'm aiming for system engineer roles. My project, a smart attendance system, uses a camera and a small database to mark attendance automatically, and it won first prize at our college expo. I was also the coordinator of our technical club's workshop series.
>
> These experiences taught me to learn new tools quickly and work in a team. Right now I'm building hands-on skills in Linux and networking by setting up my own small lab. I'm looking for a role where I can keep learning and contribute from day one. Thank you.

Keep the exact names, dates and **your own role** in every project ready for follow-up questions.

## 4. Grammar slips to avoid in introductions

These are common across many candidates. Check your own script against them.

| Common phrasing | Better | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| "Myself Ravi." | "My name is Ravi." | "Myself" is not a subject. |
| "I am having 8 CGPA." | "I have a CGPA of 8." | Do not use "having" for possession. |
| "I am pursuing BTech third year in X." | "I'm a third-year BTech student at X." | Name the status with a full, simple verb. |
| "I did a project in machine learning." | "I worked on a machine learning project." | Natural verb choice. |
| "I am passionate about many roles." | Name one focus. | Focus beats a long list. |
| "My strength is I am hardworking." | "My strength is that I am hardworking, for example..." | Add "that", then give proof. |
| "We discussed about the topic." | "We discussed the topic." | "Discuss" needs no "about". |
| "I also been part of a team." | "I was also part of a team." | Use "was" or "have been", not bare "been". |

Also: use **active voice** ("Our project won a gold medal") and show qualities through examples instead of claiming them ("I am adaptable" is empty without proof).

## 5. Fillers ("uh", "um", "like", "you know", "basically")

**What they are:** sounds we make while the brain searches for the next word.
**Why they happen:** nerves, speaking too fast, translating in your head, or not knowing what comes next.

**The fix (practise all five):**
1. **Pause instead of filling.** A silent one-second pause sounds confident. A filler sounds nervous.
2. **Slow down** to about 120 to 150 words per minute.
3. **Speak in short chunks.** One idea per sentence.
4. **Know your first sentence of each part by heart.** Starts are where fillers cluster.
5. **Record and count.** Target: 3 or fewer fillers in 90 seconds.

**Useful bridge phrases** (use sparingly): "That's a good question." / "Let me think for a moment." / "To give you an example..." / "In my experience..."

## 6. The most repeated HR questions (campus and off-campus)

> Honest note: these are patterns that repeat in HR rounds across service companies and consulting firms. They are not an exact list of any company's questions.

| # | Question | What the interviewer wants | Answer framework |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell me about yourself | Clarity and fit | Present -> proof -> skills -> future |
| 2 | What are your strengths? | Relevant, proven strengths | Strength + one example |
| 3 | What are your weaknesses? | Self-awareness | Real but fixable weakness + what you are doing about it |
| 4 | Why do you want to join our company? | You researched | Company strength + your skill match |
| 5 | Why should we hire you? | Value to them | Skills + proof + attitude |
| 6 | Where do you see yourself in 5 years? | Ambition and stability | Skill growth in the same field |
| 7 | Tell me about your project and your role | Real contribution | Problem -> your part -> result |
| 8 | Are you willing to relocate or work in shifts? | Flexibility | Honest, positive answer |
| 9 | Tell me about a challenge you solved | Problem-solving | **STAR** |
| 10 | Tell me about teamwork or a conflict | Collaboration | **STAR** |
| 11 | Why system engineer / cloud, not development? | Genuine motivation for an infrastructure-style role | See model below |
| 12 | Do you have questions for us? | Interest | Ask about team, training, growth |
| 13 | What do you know about our company? | Preparation | 3 facts about the company |
| 14 | Describe yourself in three words | Self-image | Three traits + one-line proof each |

### STAR method (for questions 9 and 10)
- **S**ituation: the context in one sentence.
- **T**ask: what you had to do.
- **A**ction: what **you** did (use "I", not "we").
- **R**esult: the outcome, with numbers if you can.

### Model answer framework for question 11
"I enjoy building and running the systems that keep applications reliable, not only writing application code. In my projects I saw that a good idea only works when it is deployed, monitored and kept running. That interested me in Linux, cloud and automation, and I'm now learning these hands-on. A system engineer role lets me work on exactly this."

Make it true: only claim what you can demonstrate in an interview.

### Model answer framework for question 3 (weakness)
Pick a real, non-critical weakness and show you are fixing it. Structure: "I used to [weakness]. I noticed it when [situation]. I'm working on it by [action], and now [progress]."

Example: "I used to spend too long perfecting small details and sometimes missed deadlines. I noticed it during a group project. Now I set a time limit for each task and review at the end, and I deliver on time."

Avoid fake weaknesses ("I work too hard") and weaknesses that disqualify you for the role.

## 7. Practice (30 minutes)

1. Fill in the template above and read it aloud 3 times. After each take, count the fillers.
2. Write 4 to 5 sentences each for questions **2, 3 and 11**.
3. Read one editorial and write 5 new words, each with your own sentence.

## 8. Expected practice questions (answer aloud, then write)

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. What is your biggest strength? Give one example.
3. What is your biggest weakness? What are you doing about it?
4. Why do you want to be a system engineer or cloud engineer?
5. Tell me about your role in your best project.
6. Describe a challenge in one of your projects and how you solved it (use STAR).
7. Where do you see yourself in five years?
8. Are you comfortable with night shifts and relocation?
